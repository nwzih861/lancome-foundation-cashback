## Matching the formula to your skin, not to the marketing

The honest split is oil versus dryness, and how much you care about the finish.

**If you're oily or combination:** the matte one. Reviewers on Sephora's UK and US listings describe wearing Ultra Wear through Miami heat and full working days without it sliding or needing powder. That's the consistent theme in the positive reviews — longevity and oil control first, coverage second.

The flip side shows up just as often. One reviewer with combination-oily skin and fine lines said the finish held up but her skin felt drier than usual and she needed a hydrating finishing spray and multiple reapplications. Another said it didn't sit well over cream blush and had to be tapped on top rather than applied normally. Matte foundations do that; it isn't a defect, it's the trade.

**If you're normal to dry:** Care & Glow. The formula is 82% hydrating serum with hyaluronic and mandelic acid, and the whole pitch is a plumped, luminous finish rather than a flat one. Mandelic acid is the interesting part — it's an exfoliating acid, so this is one of the few foundations that's genuinely trying to do something to your skin over weeks rather than just sit on it. Lancôme's own consumer study, quoted on Sephora's product page, says 9 out of 10 of the 107 women who tried it for three weeks reported more even-looking skin tone.

Treat that number the way you'd treat any brand's three-week study. It's self-reported, it's the brand's own, and there was no control group.

**If you're sensitive or acne-prone:** both product pages claim suitability — Ultra Wear's copy specifically says it doesn't dry, clog or crease, and Care & Glow is described as suitable for sensitive skin and free of a long list of common irritants. The one complaint that shows up across formulas isn't irritation, it's fragrance. Multiple reviewers describe a strong perfume-like scent. If you react to fragrance in base makeup, that's your real risk factor here, not the acids.

## The shade codes, and why your old shade number doesn't exist anymore

Lancôme shades read like 120N, 245C, 305N, 540C. The number tracks depth, the letter tracks undertone — C for cool, N for neutral, W for warm. The fuller version, like "120N (fair with neutral undertone)" or "540C (deep with cool red undertones)", is spelled out on the retailer product pages, so you can decode any code on a swatch chart before ordering.

The relaunch changed the numbering, which is where people get caught. One Sephora UK reviewer who'd worn Maquimat in Beige Albatre bought 245C and found "before 01 Beige Albatre" printed on the box — Lancôme is mapping old shade names onto the new codes on the packaging itself. If you know your old Lancôme shade, look for that cross-reference rather than guessing from swatches on a screen.

For olive and deeper skin tones, Lancôme says the reformulation uses oxide green and ultramarine blue pigments specifically to improve the fit, and claims 9 out of 10 people find a match across the 50-shade range. Ultramarine blue is the standard trick for cutting the orange cast that ruins deep shades, so there's substance behind that claim even if the "9 out of 10" figure comes from the brand.

Practical move: order a sample or a small size first, or get matched in store, then buy the full bottle online. Thirty millilitres of the wrong undertone is an expensive mistake, and cashback doesn't pay you back on a shade you won't wear.

## What reviews actually complain about

Reading through the Sephora UK reviews — several dated through 2026 — the pattern in the negative ones is narrow and specific:

- **The lid.** One reviewer's cap came loose and stopped screwing on, which matters if it lives in a makeup bag.
- **Fragrance.** Described as strong and perfume-like by more than one person, including reviewers who otherwise liked the result.
- **Drying on drier skin types.** Matte finish doing matte things.
- **Layering.** Cream blush over the top didn't behave; powder blush did.

And a caveat worth knowing before you weigh up star ratings: several of the most detailed reviews on Sephora's listing are marked as received in exchange for an honest review, and Lancôme's own site carries incentivised reviews too. The ultra-detailed, evenly-balanced five-star write-ups are often gifted-product reviews. They're still useful for texture and wear descriptions, less useful as an independent verdict.

The Middle East Sephora listing for Ultra Wear shows 4.5 stars across 2,468 reviews, and the recurring praise is shade match and all-day hold on oily skin. Nothing in the reviews suggests the formula is controversial. It's a well-liked matte foundation with a strong scent and a lid people complain about.

## What it costs, and why the list price isn't the real price

The two reference points that are stable enough to plan around:

- Care & Glow: **US$53.00** for 1 oz at Sephora and Ulta.
- Ultra Wear: **£41.00** for 30 ml at Sephora UK.

Both fluctuate by market, and Sephora's own Auto-Replenish option takes 5% off Care & Glow, bringing it to US$50.35 — worth knowing if you already know your shade and go through a bottle every few months.

Beyond that, the price is genuinely unstable. Department stores and TV retail routinely undercut the list price, and third-party beauty retailers discount it too. The point isn't that there's one secret cheapest shop; it's that paying full list price for a 30 ml bottle is optional.
